<h1>Essays &amp; Legacy</h1>

<blockquote>
  "Authentically digital." The phrase that defined a design language that dared
  to look like nothing else on the market.
</blockquote>

<p>Windows Phone died and took a design language with it. These essays are my
attempt to write down what it actually was, not the nostalgic highlight reel,
but the ideas underneath, with sources.</p>

<h2>Planned essays</h2>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Why Metro mattered</strong>: typography-first design, honest
  interfaces, glanceability; the arc from Zune through WP7, 8, 8.1, and the
  quiet sunset. <em>(in progress)</em></li>
  <li><strong>The hardware</strong>: love letters to the phones themselves:
  polycarbonate bodies, true blacks on AMOLED, and the glorious absurdity of a
  41-megapixel camera in your pocket. Written by someone who carries one daily.
  <em>(planned)</em></li>
  <li><strong>Primary sources</strong>: an annotated index of archived Microsoft
  design documentation (wayback captures of the Metro language PDFs and friends),
  UI kits, press materials from the era. <em>(planned)</em></li>
</ul>

<p>This section is deliberately open. If you have footage, screenshots, or just
memories of the platform, contributions are welcome as ordinary PRs. The phones
are gone; the record of why they mattered shouldn't be.</p>
